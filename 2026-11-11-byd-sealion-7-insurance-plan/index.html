Why do Essential and Plus cost more but cover less?

The tiers aren't one ladder. They're two families:

  • Assistance family (Basic → Advanced → Pro → Signature → Elite): each step adds help services. Most are cheap for ICICI to provide: phone help, referrals, arranging things.
  • Courtesy-car family (Essential, Plus): basic roadside help + one expensive benefit, a courtesy car or daily allowance while your car is repaired. That's real money for ICICI, so the price is higher even though the list is short. And it's limited to 1 day (8 hrs / 80 km), so it's poor value.

What Signature doesn't have: emergency driver assist, the courtesy car and the one-time convenience service.

Only ICICI's own pages list these benefits. Each has limits and conditions in the policy wording, e.g. towing up to 50 km, minor repairs up to ₹2,000, hotel up to ₹5,000.

1+3 or 3+3?

First: what does "1+3" actually mean?

A car policy has two parts. Third party covers damage you cause to other people, and the law requires 3 years of it for a new car. Own damage covers your car, and all the add-ons (zero dep, battery, RTI, Smart Assist) attach to it.

1+3 = own damage for 1 year + third party for 3 years. If you did nothing after year 1, only third party would remain and your car would have no cover. So you renew own damage every year, which is just buying it again for another year (10 minutes online, all add-ons included).

Zero depreciation and Battery Protect: why both?

They cover different problems. Zero dep makes an already covered claim pay in full. Battery Protect adds cover for battery damage that a normal policy doesn't cover at all.

Zero depreciation, ₹15,922

Normally, when a part is replaced after an accident, the insurer deducts "depreciation": up to 50% on plastic, rubber, and batteries, and more on metal as the car ages. You pay that difference. Zero dep removes the deduction.

Only works when the damage itself is covered: accident, fire, theft, flood, riots and so on.

Battery Protect, ₹11,544

ICICI: covers "repair or replacement expenses for damages caused by water ingress or short circuits to the battery".

Those are usually excluded from a normal policy as "consequential" or "electrical breakdown" damage, the same way petrol cars need Engine Protect for water damage.

Third party: what it covers, and what happens after 3 years

Covers
  • Injury or death of other people caused by your car: no upper limit, as decided by the court
  • Damage to other people's property (their car, wall, shop): up to ₹7.5 lakh
  • Legal liability to your paid driver (already in the ICICI quote)
Does not cover
  • Any damage to your own car: that's the own-damage part
  • Your own injuries: that's personal accident cover
  • Theft, fire or flood damage to your car

What to do if it ever happens to you

  1. Keep Return to Invoice in your policy. If the car is declared a total loss, you get the full invoice price, not the lower IDV.
  2. Don't agree to a repair under pressure. That was his own admitted mistake.
  3. Get it all in writing: the dealer's estimate, the surveyor's report and its reasoning, and the insurer's decision.
  4. For an EV, ask for a written battery health report from BYD (capacity and range) before you accept a repaired car.
  5. Drive the repaired car for a week before you sign off. He only found the range problem on a highway trip.
  6. If you disagree, escalate in order: ICICI grievance cell → IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal → Insurance Ombudsman (free) → consumer court. You can also ask for an independent surveyor.
Does this change the recommendation? No. The alternatives don't remove this risk: HDFC ERGO costs much more, Liberty covers less on a lower car value, and Tata AIG, Digit and Acko won't insure the car at all. What protects you is Return to Invoice + written records + knowing the 75% rule, with any insurer.

Should you buy the extension?

Yes, if the car will do under ~18,000 km a year. Then it covers all 8 years for about ₹9,400 a year, and one screen, AC or ADAS repair can cost more than that. At 25,000+ km a year it runs out around year 6, and for a very busy company car (40,000 km a year) it adds almost nothing.

Any own-damage claim, even ₹2,000, resets your No Claim Bonus to 0%. A ₹3,000 claim in year 1 can cost you ~₹12,000–15,000 in lost discounts over the next few years (estimate).
