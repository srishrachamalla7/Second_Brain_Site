Out-of-Stock Requests

A proposal for Esszo — 30 August 2026

The problem, in one line

When something is out of stock, the person who wants it has no way to say so — and the person who could buy it has no way to know.

Today an out-of-stock product simply becomes un-orderable. The demand disappears. Nobody records that three distributors and eleven customers wanted it this week, so restocking is done on memory and instinct rather than evidence.

This proposal turns that lost demand into a buying list.

What happens today

   Distributor wants 100 kg          Consumer wants 1 kg
   Admin has 20                      Distributor has 0
            │                                 │
            ▼                                 ▼
   ┌──────────────────┐              ┌──────────────────┐

   └────────┬─────────┘              └────────┬─────────┘
            │                                 │
            ▼                                 ▼
      nothing recorded                  nothing recorded
      nobody is told                    nobody is told

The information exists for a moment, then is thrown away.

Case 1 — Distributor wants more than Admin has

Admin holds 20. A distributor needs 100.

The order is not placed for the extra 80. Stock never goes into the negative and nothing is promised that cannot be delivered. What changes is that the shortfall is now recorded instead of discarded, and Admin is told.

            ▼

   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │   ADMIN — Procurement list                  │
   │                                             │
   │   Almonds 1 kg     have 20   wanted 100     │
   │   Ghee 500 ml      have  0   wanted  40     │
   │   Cashew W320 1 kg have  5   wanted  25     │
   │                                             │
   │   [ Hold the 20 ]      [ Release the 20 ]   │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Admin sources the goods themselves — that part stays outside the system, as it is now.

The decision this screen unlocks is the one Admin cannot make today, because they cannot see the demand:

ChoiceWhat it means
HoldKeep the 20 aside for the distributor who is waiting, so the full 100 ships together
ReleaseLet other distributors order the 20 now, and send the rest when it arrives

Case 2 — Consumer wants something their distributor does not have

The consumer cannot order it. They can only say "I need this."

That signal goes to their distributor, who sees how many people are asking.

The distributor no longer hunts for each product by hand. They see the demand, tick what is worth stocking, and press once.

When both are out of stock, the signal travels

If the distributor's customers want something that Admin does not have either, both sides are told — and Admin gets the warning before the order even arrives.

   7 customers ask their distributor
              │
              ▼
   Distributor is notified
              │
              ▼
   Admin is notified early:
   "7 customers are asking for this.
    A distributor is likely to order it soon."
              │
              ▼
   Admin can buy ahead of the demand
   instead of reacting to a shortage

This is the part that changes how the business runs. Today Admin learns about demand when an order fails. Here they learn about it while there is still time to act.

What we have already started

We have completed the technical assessment ahead of this conversation, so the discussion can be about what you want rather than whether it is possible.

DoneFinding
Reviewed the existing systemStock is already tracked precisely at both levels — Admin's central inventory and each distributor's own stock. The information needed for these screens already exists.
Confirmed the approachBecause stock never goes negative and no order is forced through, no major change to the existing ordering or inventory rules is required. This was the main risk, and it is not there.
Identified where it connectsThe distributor-to-Admin restock order already exists, so the one-press reorder plugs into a proven flow rather than a new one.
Checked for overlapThe system has two existing "request" features with similar names. Neither does this job, so there is no conflict and nothing to unpick.
Agreed the level of detailRequests are recorded per variant — 1 kg and 250 g are separate signals — so the buying list is accurate about pack sizes.

We have also just delivered Counter Sales, the walk-in billing screen, on both Esszo and Bi2Bi — including a GST invoice, automatic stock deduction and accounting. A full user manual for it is available.

What we need from you

Six decisions. None of them technical — each one changes how the feature behaves for your team, so we would rather ask than assume.

#QuestionWhy it matters
1When does a distributor pay for goods that are not in stock yet — when they order, or when it ships?Decides when the sale is recorded in the accounts.
2Should there be a limit on how much a distributor can ask for beyond what is available?Without one, a single large request can dominate the buying list.
3If the item cannot be sourced at all, who tells the distributor, and what happens to the order?Needs an agreed process, not just a screen.
4When new stock arrives, should it be given automatically to whoever was waiting, or should Admin choose?Automatic is faster. Choosing gives Admin control over who gets scarce stock.
5Should a request expire? Is somebody who asked six weeks ago still counted today?Keeps the buying list honest rather than slowly filling with old demand.
6If the same customer asks repeatedly, is that one voice or several?Otherwise one enthusiastic customer can look like ten, and the wrong thing gets bought.

Still being decided on our side

Notifications. We know the direction — the distributor is told when customers ask, and customers are told when the item arrives. What we have not settled is how: an in-app message, a push notification, or SMS. We will come back to you on this.

Suggested order of work

   STEP 1  ── Customer requests + distributor's one-press reorder
              Adds new screens without changing any existing rule.
              This is where most of the day-to-day value is.

   STEP 2  ── Admin procurement list + hold / release
              Needs decisions 1-4 above before it can start.

   STEP 3  ── Notifications
              Last, once the rest is in use and the channel is agreed.

We recommend starting with Step 1. It is self-contained, it can go live on its own, and it is the part your distributors will feel immediately.

In short

  • Demand that is currently lost gets recorded
  • Admin gets a buying list driven by real orders and real customers
  • Distributors get a one-press reorder instead of hunting through the catalogue
  • Nothing is over-promised — stock is never shown as available when it is not
  • The groundwork is done; six answers from you and we can begin

Questions on any of this are welcome — that is what this document is for.
