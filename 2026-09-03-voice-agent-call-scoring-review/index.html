Case by case

The one-minute silence — pass, but a real gap

CALLER:  "wait for one minute"
           |
           v
AGENT:   [silent]        for a full minute
           |
           v
CALLER:  hangs up

Scored pass — the caller ended it, not the agent. But Srish flagged the underlying hole: "How can we solve this? Because agent doesn't know the time." The agent has no clock, so it cannot tell a one-minute hold from an abandoned call, and cannot come back and say "are you still there?".

This is very likely the same defect the client is describing from the other side as "it repeatedly says 'Please wait for 5 seconds'" — an agent with no sense of elapsed time either sits silent or fills the gap by repeating itself.

The design decision — one tool, not two

The biggest product change agreed on the call. Today, when a doctor is not available at the branch the caller asked for, the agent asks whether another branch is acceptable. Srish wants that dead-end question removed and the caller handed to a human when nothing is found anywhere.

BEFORE
  caller wants a doctor at Uppal
        |
  not available at Uppal
        |
  agent: "will you see at Miyapur branch?"      <- dead-end question
        |
  caller: ...?

AFTER  (one tool: suggest_doctor, searching ALL branches)
  caller wants a doctor at Uppal
        |
  suggest_doctor  ->  searches Uppal + the other 3 branches
        |
        +-- found at Banjara Hills   ->  "not at Uppal, but available at
        |                                 Banjara Hills — is that OK?"
        |
        +-- found nowhere            ->  TRANSFER to receptionist. No question.
"రిసెప్షనిస్ట్ కి... డైరెక్ట్ ట్రాన్స్ఫర్ చేసేయాలి." Instead of asking "will you see at Miyapur branch", just transfer directly to the receptionist.
"ఒక టూల్ లోనే ఎలా చేయొచ్చు అంటే, సజెస్ట్ డాక్టర్ ఉంటుంది సర్... ఇంకా త్రీ బ్రాంచెస్ లో అదే టూల్ అయిపోతుంది. మళ్లీ ఇంకో టూల్ కావాల్సిన అవసరం లేదు." It can be done in one tool — there is a suggest-doctor tool. If the concern isn't covered at Uppal, put the other branches in it too. The same tool covers the other three branches. No second tool is needed.

The trade-off was named out loud, not hidden:

"బట్ దీంట్లో ఫెయిల్స్ ఎక్కువ వస్తాయి సర్, కంపారిటివ్ గా." But with this, more fails will come, comparatively.

Handing off early is honest, but it scores as a non-conversion. Which loops straight back to the client's "not even a single conversion" — the metric punishes the safe behaviour. If a transfer to a human is not counted as a success, the right fix looks like a regression on the report.

What goes back to the client

Not a defence — a sequence. The order matters.

1.  "55 calls total. 34 are leads."          <- one line, UP FRONT
2.  "Of the 34: 10 booked, 10 transferred."
3.  Per-call report, with a reason on every non-conversion.
4.  "And we are giving a new build — some of these are already fixed."

Srish drafted the closing line twice on the call, and deliberately softened it the second time:

"నాట్ ఓన్లీ దట్, వి ఆర్ వర్కింగ్ ఆన్ న్యూ ఏజెంట్, అండ్ ఇన్ ద న్యూ ఏజెంట్, ఆల్ ఆఫ్ దిస్ విల్ బి ఫిక్స్డ్." Not only that — we are working on a new agent, and in the new agent all of this will be fixed.
"లేదు సర్, కొత్త బిల్డ్ ఇస్తున్నాం, దాంట్లో ఆల్రెడీ కొన్ని ఫిక్స్ అయిపోయినాయి అని చెప్దాం అనుకుంటున్నా." No sir — I think we should say we are giving a new build, and some things are already fixed in it.

"All of this will be fixed" is a promise. "Some are already fixed" is a fact. He chose the fact.
